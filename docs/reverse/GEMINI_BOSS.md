# Gemini Boss

The Gemini family is the second normal pre-Drone boss selected when one Drone outcome has already been processed. The family is established by a dedicated initializer, loader/releaser pair, paired body/head combat update, and asset/audio namespace. Phase 4 now owns its shareware gameplay combat natively; immutable body/head frame pixels remain the collision-asset input and the randomized presentation tail remains separate.

## Functions

| address | research name | evidence |
|---:|---|---|
| `0x00405000` | `update_gemini_boss` | shared-root movement, opposing body animation, bomb emission, head/body special collision, damage thresholds, destruction tails and randomized effects |
| `0x00405EF0` | `initialize_gemini_boss` | selected by boss dispatch slot 1; activates two body/head pairs and initializes motion/audio |
| `0x00405FB0` | `load_gemini_boss_assets` | loads Gemini JBA/WAV family and builds frame banks |
| `0x00406190` | `release_gemini_boss_assets` | releases the same entity frames/audio and clears load guard |

Resource load guard: `0x004D95BC`.

## Entities and frame sharing

Global entity construction establishes two 56×41 body entities and two 43×34 head entities:

- body A: `0x00467538`;
- body B: `0x00467690`;
- head A: `0x00464D40`;
- head B: `0x00464E98`.

`gemini1.jba` supplies body frame slots 0..14 in a 5×3 guttered grid. `gemini2.jba` supplies slots 15..29 in the same layout. The loader constructs the 30-frame bank on body A and shares/copies the frame-pointer table into body B rather than decoding a second copy of the artwork. `gemhead.jba` supplies the 43×34 head frame and the second head shares that recovered frame pointer.

Metadata-only hashes for all 31 unique extracted frames are generated by `scripts/analyze_sprite_sheets.py` and stored in `manifests/recovered_sprite_frames.csv`.

## Native initialization and geometry

`0x00405EF0` activates both bodies and both heads, clears the two head-damage accumulators and initializes the shared body-A motion root:

- body A integer position `(0,-100)`;
- body A fixed position `(0,-100<<16)`;
- initial X velocity `10923 * difficulty`;
- initial Y velocity `0x4001 + 0x1555 * difficulty`;
- horizontal speed cap `(difficulty + 3) * 3 * 0x1000`.

Every update derives the remaining composite geometry from body A:

```text
body B = body A + (170, 0)
head A = body A + (6, 41)
head B = body B + (6, 41)
```

The update compares the midpoint of the two **head X origins** against player-left X. It accelerates the shared X velocity by `+/-0x44C` and clamps it to the difficulty-scaled cap. Once integer body-A/root Y reaches 240, `0x004050BA..0x004050F9` increments shared byte `0x00454B04`, optionally plays `level2.wav` when the byte reaches 8, then writes `0xFF9C0000` to fixed-Y field `0x00467544`. The vertical velocity at `0x0046754C` is untouched, so Gemini wraps to Y=-100 and continues descending rather than entering an upward retreat. The initializer clears the shared cadence byte at `0x00405F87`. `level2.wav` is a one-shot (`Play` flags 0) every eighth completed traversal.

On gameplay phase 2, body A's current frame increments and wraps 30→0 while body B runs in the opposite direction, decrementing 0→29.

## Traversal audio cadence

The normally reachable shareware bosses reuse one process/global cadence byte at `0x00454B04`, but their initializers clear it before each encounter, so the counter does not leak between Lid/Top and Gemini. For Gemini, body A's shared fixed Y is `0x00467544`; each bottom crossing increments the byte, plays handle `0x00466B0C` on count 8, resets the byte to zero, and then wraps fixed Y to `-100<<16`. `0x00405FB0` loads that handle from `level2.wav` and applies volume 100.

The clean boss lifecycle owns the cadence counter because it is driven by exact traversal state, while the emitted sound remains an allocation-free semantic `AudioEvent`. This correction supersedes the earlier Phase-4 interpretation that Y>=240 changed vertical velocity to `-100<<16`.

## Native bomb attack

The phase-2 attack first consumes `rand()%100`; the draw occurs before later activity/gate/capacity rejection. A bomb attempt passes when:

```text
rand()%100 < 2*difficulty
```

and at least one body remains activity 1, the shared enemy-bomb gate equals 5, and a bomb slot is available. The first inactive bomb slot is selected. A second `rand()%10` chooses the preferred source side:

- `<5`: prefer side A;
- `>=5`: prefer side B;
- if the preferred body is no longer active, fall back to the other active side.

The bomb is positioned at `(selected_head.x + 21, selected_head.y + 33)`. Like the native Lid/Top branch, Gemini does not initialize the reused bomb's horizontal-motion/current-frame fields. The active count increments and the shared gate resets to zero.

## Native Probe/Stinger damage

The two sides have separate but symmetric collision branches. Side A executes before side B.

For each active body, a launched special is considered only while body Y is greater than `-20`. Collision uses `0x00401FA0` current-frame opaque-pixel testing in strict order:

1. the corresponding 43×34 head frame;
2. if the head pixel misses, the current 56×41 body frame.

A hit consumes the special into activity 10 and zeros its Y motion.

Damage is selected from the special kind:

- Probe: `+3`;
- Stinger: `+15` and activate the separate centered six-frame `stinger.jba` display at the impact point.

The damage bytes and thresholds are now explicitly mapped:

| side | damage | threshold | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | `0x00464D70` | `0x00464D71` | 20 | 35 | 35 |
| B | `0x00464EC8` | `0x00464EC9` | 20 | 30 | 30 |

The comparison is strict: destruction begins only when `damage > threshold`, not when equal. Therefore side B at higher difficulty survives exactly 30 points and transitions on the next damaging hit.

Because side A consumes a colliding special before side B's state-3 gate is reached, one launched Probe/Stinger cannot damage both Gemini sides in one boss update.

## Destruction and scoring

Threshold crossing independently for either side:

- sets that body's activity to 2;
- resets its body `+0x32` destruction counter to zero;
- immediately awards **+100** to total score and extra-life progress;
- leaves the corresponding head activity byte untouched.

The activity-2 counter advances only on gameplay phase 2 and clears that body at exactly **20** ticks. The retirement block occurs before the same side's active collision branch, so a body newly transitioned to activity 2 starts at counter zero and does not consume its first retirement tick until a later phase-2 update.

The executable also temporarily saves/zeros/restores motion fields while emitting destruction visuals. For side A, those are body A's shared-root velocity fields; the saved values are restored before side-B processing, so the apparent zeroing is **not** a permanent movement stop. The clean gameplay owner therefore keeps the shared root moving while presentation effects remain separate.

Gemini can award **+200 total** when both sides independently cross their thresholds. It must not be simplified to one generic boss-killed callback.

## Stinger targeting integration

The red-Stinger selector now consumes Gemini geometry directly from the session-owned pre-boss-update snapshot. When both bodies are active, it chooses the head whose X origin is nearest player-left X; equal distance selects head B. No host-supplied Gemini geometry remains in `GameSessionTargetContext`.

## Presentation and RNG boundary

`0x00405000` contains substantial randomized point-particle/destruction-effect work, and the renderer contains the Gemini-specific procedural block at `0x00410514..0x004107AE`. When both halves are active, that renderer derives the horizontal interval between the heads and paints a randomized palette-index band; when the active player lies inside the interval it performs a much heavier locked-surface effect.

These presentation paths call the same CRT PRNG used by gameplay. Phase 4 currently owns Gemini's gameplay RNG draws for bomb chance/source selection, but it does **not yet consume every randomized particle/beam/debris draw** from the presentation paths. Thus Gemini movement, attack choice, target geometry, damage thresholds and destruction timing are native, while exact cross-subsystem RNG sequencing during presentation-heavy Gemini frames remains a fidelity boundary.

The clean evidence name `GeminiProceduralBeam` describes geometry/ownership only; it is not asserted to be original developer terminology. See [`WORLD_PRESENTATION_SUBPASSES.md`](WORLD_PRESENTATION_SUBPASSES.md).

## Evidence-set boundary

Unlike later registered bosses, the Gemini graphics/audio required by the dedicated loader are present in the supplied shareware installation. That is consistent with the explicit two-level shareware campaign: Gemini is the second normally reachable boss family.
